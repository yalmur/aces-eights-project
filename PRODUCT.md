# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences in roughly equal weight. Local regulars order on mobile for fast repeat delivery/collection — they know the menu and want minimal friction. First-time or occasional visitors are discovering the shop (search, social, walking past) and need the menu, customization options, and trust signals made clear before they commit to an order. Both groups meet on the same surfaces (menu, cart, checkout), so neither can be optimized for at the other's expense — the ordering flow needs to be fast for people who already know what they want, while still legible to someone seeing the menu for the first time.

## Product Purpose

A full-stack ordering platform for a single pizza takeaway shop (Aces & Eights Pizza, London), covering delivery, collection, eat-in, table booking, and party hall booking for 50+ guests. The core commercial goal is shifting order volume from phone calls to the website/app — every screen exists to move a customer from "hungry" to "order placed" without needing to pick up the phone. Success looks like fewer phone orders, fewer order-taking errors, and a menu/customization experience good enough that regulars default to it.

## Positioning

Deep, correct customization (toppings, sizes, crusts, smart filters, search) done properly — not a generic fixed-menu checkout bolted onto a template. The site should feel like it belongs to one real shop with its own kitchen and character, not a marketplace listing among many.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy and reliable first — orders must go through correctly, pricing must be transparent, nothing surprises the customer at checkout. Bold and craft-forward — the oxblood-red/gold identity should be used with confidence, not diluted into a generic template look. Warm and neighborhood — this reads as the corner shop people already know, not a faceless chain app. Reference lane: premium indie-restaurant sites (confident typography, real photography, a distinct point of view).

## Anti-references

Avoid the generic food-delivery marketplace look (stock Uber-Eats-style templates with no distinct identity). This is one shop's own site, not a marketplace listing — lean into the premium indie-restaurant reference above rather than default SaaS/food-delivery scaffolding.

## Design Principles

Ordering over browsing: every screen should move the customer toward completing an order, not just admiring the menu.

Customization is the product, not an afterthought: topping/size/crust choices need to feel deep and effortless, since that's the stated differentiator over generic ordering templates.

One shop, not a marketplace: presence and craft-forward identity win over generic marketplace neutrality.

Operational reliability is part of the UX: kitchen display, admin, and delivery-zone tooling failures become customer-facing failures (wrong orders, late delivery), so back-of-house surfaces get the same rigor as customer-facing ones.

Familiar over novel: regulars should feel at home and move fast; first-time visitors should be onboarded to the menu and customization options quickly, without a steep learning curve.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: 4.5:1 text contrast minimum, full keyboard navigation, `prefers-reduced-motion` support. No additional known user needs beyond baseline good practice.
